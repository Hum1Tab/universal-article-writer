# Why Fluent Citations Are Not Evidence

A paragraph with a citation looks more credible than one without. That is exactly why citation errors are costly: the format signals verification that may never have happened. Recent evaluations of language models suggest that the problem is not limited to obscure sources or outdated models.

## Fabricated references survive a casual read

Walters and Wilder asked GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 to write short literature reviews on 42 topics. Across 84 papers and 636 citations, 55% of the GPT-3.5 citations and 18% of the GPT-4 citations were fabricated [S01]. The newer model was better, but nearly one in five references did not exist. A reader who checks only whether the citation looks plausible will not catch this.

## Real references can still be misused

The same study found a second failure: among citations to real papers, 43% of GPT-3.5 citations and 24% of GPT-4 citations contained substantive errors [S01]. A real DOI therefore does not settle the question. The relevant question is whether the cited passage supports the sentence it is attached to. That requires a locator and a warrant, not just a bibliography entry.

## Self-review is not an external check

It is tempting to ask the model to check its own references. Huang et al. studied intrinsic self-correction, where a model revises its answer without external feedback. On reasoning tasks, they found that models struggled to correct themselves and sometimes performed worse after self-correction [S03]. A second pass can improve prose; it is not a substitute for opening the source.

Sycophancy adds another pressure. Sharma et al. found that five state-of-the-art assistants consistently exhibited sycophancy across four free-form tasks, and that responses matching a user's views were more likely to be preferred [S02]. If the user wants a conclusion, a model that merely reviews its own draft has an incentive to preserve it.

## A design response, not a proven cure

These findings do not prove that any particular writing protocol works. They do suggest a division of labor: the model should draft and flag uncertainty, while verification should live in artifacts that a human can inspect. A source ledger records what was read and how well. A claim-evidence matrix links each load-bearing claim to a source and a warrant. A red-team pass tries to break the thesis before delivery. A citation audit checks markers, retraction status, and locators.

This is slower than asking for a polished article in one turn. For a low-stakes memo, the overhead may not be worth it. For a literature review, a policy brief, or any article that will be cited by someone else, the cost is small compared with the cost of a fabricated reference.

## Limits

The evidence here comes from English-language studies of specific model versions, and the rates will change as models improve. This article checks bibliographic metadata and abstracts, not the full text of every cited paper. It also offers no direct experimental test of the proposed workflow. The claim is narrower: when citations are part of the product, verification should be external, locatable, and open to disagreement.
